#### **Scene 3.0**

1. **For student and early-career work, how should you treat Generative AI?**  
   a) As a source of truth that replaces banks and official portals  
   b) As a person who can sit exams and sign legal papers for you  
   c) As a draft partner whose output you still own and must check  
   d) As a replacement for all traditional software in payments and bookings  

   **Answer:** c) As a draft partner whose output you still own and must check  

   **Explanation:** AI can help with writing, study, first-pass research, analysis of given text, planning, language, and brainstorming. You remain the author who submits, sends, or publishes.

2. **What is the difference between an unreliable AI task and an unsuitable one?**  
   a) Unreliable work is illegal; unsuitable work is always safe to run  
   b) Unreliable means never use it; unsuitable means skip all checking  
   c) Both labels mean the model is always correct on the first try  
   d) Unreliable: verify first; unsuitable: another system or do not paste  

   **Answer:** d) Unreliable: verify first; unsuitable: another system or do not paste  

   **Explanation:** Unreliable means you may still use the tool if you verify (live facts, citations, local data). Unsuitable means a different system, a professional, or “do not paste that.”

3. **Why should you not trust a basic chat reply for this hour’s petrol price in your city?**  
   a) Live figures need an official or primary source, not a generated guess  
   b) Language models cannot output any text that looks like a number  
   c) Petrol prices are stored as fingerprints on the phone unlock sensor  
   d) The model always refuses to write any text that resembles a price  

   **Answer:** a) Live figures need an official or primary source, not a generated guess  

   **Explanation:** Live facts are a typical unreliable use. The model can still write a template; you fill the real number from an official board, app, or site.

4. **Which request is unsuitable rather than merely unreliable?**  
   a) Asking for a first-draft event outline that you will still edit  
   b) Pasting login passwords and unpublished customer lists into the chat  
   c) Requesting several conversation-starter ideas for a networking evening  
   d) Requesting a clearer rewrite of meeting notes you already typed  

   **Answer:** b) Pasting login passwords and unpublished customer lists into the chat  

   **Explanation:** Secrets, high-stakes advice you will act on blindly, cheating, and harm are unsuitable. Drafts, brainstorming, and rewriting text you supplied are usually well-suited if you edit.

5. **Before you send AI-written text, what does checking accuracy mainly involve?**  
   a) Counting how many adjectives the model used in each paragraph  
   b) Assuming specific details are true because they sound confident  
   c) Verifying numbers, dates, names, and claims against a primary source  
   d) Changing the font so the draft looks more official on the page  

   **Answer:** c) Verifying numbers, dates, names, and claims against a primary source  

   **Explanation:** Accuracy asks whether a claim is true. Over-specific details are not proof. Check hard facts against an official page, your notes, or a person who knows.
