# Foundation Course Syllabus

---

## Session 1

**Session Name:** AI Fundamentals: What It Is, How to Prompt It, What It Can Do

**Objective of Session:** Build a practical foundation in artificial intelligence and large language models so learners can use AI tools confidently at work. The session covers how AI differs from traditional software, how models produce text, and how to write clear prompts and judge when AI output is trustworthy.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Explain in plain language what AI and large language models are, and how they differ from traditional software.
- Describe how LLMs generate responses and define tokenization as the way text is processed by a model.
- Explain what generative AI is and write structured prompts using a Role–Context–Format approach.
- Identify what makes a prompt effective (specificity, constraints, output format) and improve vague prompts.
- Map tasks where AI helps today versus where it is unreliable, and evaluate generated output before use.

---

## Session 2

**Session Name:** Building a Product with AI — Part 1: From Idea to Prototype

**Objective of Session:** Guide learners from an initial product idea to a testable prototype using AI brainstorming and AI-assisted no-code tools. The focus is on shaping a clear concept, building a simple landing page or wireframe, and knowing when the prototype is ready to share for feedback.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Use AI to brainstorm, refine, and stress-test a product idea (target user, problem, timing).
- Translate a raw idea into a concise product concept and one-line value proposition.
- Build a basic wireframe or landing page with an AI-assisted no-code tool.
- Structure core page elements (headline, value proposition, call-to-action) and generate supporting copy and visuals.
- Distinguish a polished prototype from a testable one and define what feedback to collect when sharing it.

---

## Session 3

**Session Name:** Building a Product with AI — Part 2: Refining & Validating

**Objective of Session:** Show how to gather and use feedback— including AI-assisted simulation—to iterate toward a minimum viable product and communicate changes clearly. Learners practice prioritizing improvements under time limits and defending product decisions with evidence from tests.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Use AI to generate test scenarios and feedback prompts, and design questions that test problem–solution fit.
- Recognize the limits of simulated feedback compared with real user testing.
- Define an MVP and what it should and should not include.
- Iterate on a prototype from feedback and prioritize what to act on versus defer.
- Present a refined product idea with rationale tied to feedback and justified design or feature trade-offs.

---

## Session 4

**Session Name:** AI for Everyday Work: Analytics & Decision-Making

**Objective of Session:** Teach non-technical professionals to use AI for data summarization, trend discovery, and decision-ready reporting without coding or specialized analytics tools. Emphasis is on prompting with real datasets, verifying AI conclusions, and producing accurate executive summaries and briefs.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Input structured data or dashboards into AI to produce clear, plain-language executive summaries.
- Identify key metrics, patterns, and anomalies and check summary accuracy against the source data.
- Formulate targeted queries and prompt chains to surface trends, correlations, and outliers.
- Guide exploratory analysis through follow-up questions and filter noise to focus on business-critical insights.
- Recognize when human sanity checks are required and turn verified insights into a structured final report.

---

## Session 5

**Session Name:** Marketing Your Product — Part 1: Positioning & Messaging

**Objective of Session:** Help learners define who the product is for, articulate a differentiated value proposition, and craft a one-line positioning statement—with AI used to stress-test clarity and generate marketing copy. The session connects audience insight to taglines, headlines, and short value lines aligned with the brand.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify a target audience segment and articulate their primary pain point.
- Formulate a differentiated core value proposition and write a one-line positioning statement.
- Use AI to analyze customer profiles and stress-test positioning for clarity, uniqueness, and market fit.
- Generate varied taglines, landing-page headlines, and short value lines from the positioning statement.
- Evaluate and refine AI-generated copy for tone, clarity, audience resonance, and brand alignment.

---

## Session 6

**Session Name:** Marketing Your Product — Part 2: Building a Go-to-Market Snapshot

**Objective of Session:** Turn positioning into an executable go-to-market plan by choosing channels, drafting a one-page GTM snapshot with AI, and separating launch activities from ongoing marketing. Learners validate plans for feasibility and define metrics appropriate to each phase.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Select primary marketing channels (social, email, search, direct outreach) based on product type and audience behavior, using AI to research channel usage.
- Draft a one-page GTM snapshot covering segment, positioning, channel mix, adapted messages, activities, and metrics.
- Review AI-drafted plans for logical consistency and feasibility, and revise where constraints would break the plan.
- Explain how a time-bound launch plan differs from an ongoing plan focused on retention and growth.
- Design the shift from launch momentum to sustained acquisition and define separate success metrics for launch versus ongoing marketing.

---

## Session 7

**Session Name:** AI for Everyday Work: Streamlining Daily Tasks

**Objective of Session:** Apply AI to high-volume daily work such as meeting notes, multi-source updates, research synthesis, and recurring reports. Learners build reusable prompt workflows and a professional quality-control habit so AI output is accurate before it is shared.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Extract themes, decisions, deadlines, and owner-assigned action items from transcripts, notes, or long documents using AI.
- Compare AI summaries to source material to catch missed context, wrong owners, or omitted details.
- Audit recurring daily tasks and design reusable prompt templates and step-by-step AI workflows.
- Integrate those workflows into routines to save time while maintaining output quality.
- Apply a systematic fact-checking protocol for figures, quotes, and citations, and refine output for brand voice and professional standards.

---

## Session 8

**Session Name:** AI for Everyday Work: Writing Copy & Content

**Objective of Session:** Use AI to produce multiple content formats from one strategic brief—emails, social posts, product listings, and more—while adapting structure to each channel. The session stresses human editing for tone, audience fit, factual accuracy, and avoiding generic or misleading AI copy.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Construct goal-driven prompts to generate emails, social updates, product listings, and other campaign formats.
- Adapt message structure to channel constraints such as length limits, format, and call-to-action placement.
- Use iterative prompt chains to produce multiple creative variations for the same asset.
- Set voice and tone guidelines and edit drafts so they match a specific reader persona without losing the core message.
- Identify common AI writing issues (jargon, generic claims, invented facts), fact-check claims and details, and add human nuance before publishing.
