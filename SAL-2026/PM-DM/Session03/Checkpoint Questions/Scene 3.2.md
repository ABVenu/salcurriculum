#### **Scene 3.2**

1. **How should you name chatbot role-play when you present?**  
   a) Call it simulated feedback, not proof that users already love the offer  
   b) Call it a completed survey of everyone in the intended market as a whole  
   c) Hide the source and end with a funding story instead of one clear ask  
   d) Flip through several visual themes and treat that flip as the evidence  

   **Answer:** a) Call it simulated feedback, not proof that users already love the offer  

   **Explanation:** Say “simulated feedback” when that is the source. Do not claim users love the offer, hide the source, or replace the story with extra themes.

2. **Two findings point different ways. What belongs in the change log?**  
   a) Only the finding that matches the extra feature you already wanted to add  
   b) Neither finding, so the presentation can skip mentioning the conflict at all  
   c) Both findings and the MVP rule you used to decide the tie-break on the page  
   d) A taste-only note with no source, used to replace both of those findings  

   **Answer:** c) Both findings and the MVP rule you used to decide the tie-break on the page  

   **Explanation:** If two findings fight, record both and show the MVP tie-break. Dropping one side, skipping the conflict, or replacing them with taste is not a trustworthy log.

3. **How do you state a trade-off without it sounding like an excuse?**  
   a) Name only the downside and skip saying what the choice was meant to protect  
   b) Name both sides, link the choice to time or MVP, and name the remaining risk  
   c) Promise that the skipped feature is already live so that nobody has to worry  
   d) Replace the spoken trade-off with adjectives such as seamless and revolutionary  

   **Answer:** b) Name both sides, link the choice to time or MVP, and name the remaining risk  

   **Explanation:** Name what you chose and what you gave up, tie it to time or the MVP, and name the risk you still own. An apology, a false live claim, or adjectives is not that pattern.
