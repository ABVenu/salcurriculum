#### **Scene 3.2**

1. **A page looks expensive but the only button does not open anything. What is true?**  
   a) The prototype is testable because the theme looks complete  
   b) Friends clapping is enough evidence that the offer is understood  
   c) Adding more pages will automatically fix the missing next step  
   d) It is not testable until there is one working next step to use  

   **Answer:** d) It is not testable until there is one working next step to use  

   **Explanation:** A dead call-to-action fails the good-enough gate. Looks do not replace a button or form that actually works.

2. **Planned feedback for an early prototype is organised into which three layers?**  
   a) Understanding of the offer, desire to use it, and where they stall  
   b) Logo, colour palette, and dark mode  
   c) Payments, admin dashboard, and a city-wide launch  
   d) Testimonials, fake star ratings, and investor quotes  

   **Answer:** a) Understanding of the offer, desire to use it, and where they stall  

   **Explanation:** You plan to learn whether people get the offer, whether they want the job done, and where they stall. Decoration and fake social proof are not those layers.

3. **Someone opened the link and completed a small waitlist form. What does that count not prove by itself?**  
   a) That a person looked and that the button could be used  
   b) That they completed the small action you asked for  
   c) That the idea is already a proven, paying business  
   d) That you can later tally a simple yes, maybe, or no  

   **Answer:** c) That the idea is already a proven, paying business  

   **Explanation:** Opens, form fills, and yes/maybe/no are simple counts about interest in the offer. They are not proof of a company, a market survey, or payment.
