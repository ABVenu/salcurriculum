#### **Scene 3.1**

1. **When is an early prototype good enough to test?**  
   a) When friends clap because the template looks expensive  
   b) When a new person can understand the offer, see a next step, and react  
   c) When every extra feature, including payments, is already live  
   d) When the logo colour has been debated for several days  

   **Answer:** b) When a new person can understand the offer, see a next step, and react  

   **Explanation:** Good enough is about understanding and action, not decoration. A working next step and a way to write down a reaction matter more than polish.

2. **How does a testable prototype differ from a polished one?**  
   a) Testable means using as many animations as the builder allows  
   b) Polished means nobody has seen it, so it must already be correct  
   c) Testable means it looks finished even if the form does not open  
   d) Testable is clear and ready to show; extra decoration can wait  

   **Answer:** d) Testable is clear and ready to show; extra decoration can wait  

   **Explanation:** Polish is extra visual or feature work that does not change first learning. Testable means a stranger can get the offer and you can record a reaction.

3. **Why is putting a login wall on a first prototype a poor move?**  
   a) People then struggle with signup, not with whether they get the offer  
   b) Login is the only way a stranger can read a headline on the page  
   c) Every waitlist is invalid unless it starts with a one-time password  
   d) Website builders refuse to publish any page that has no login  

   **Answer:** a) People then struggle with signup, not with whether they get the offer  

   **Explanation:** Login blocks the test. You learn about account friction instead of whether the offer is understood. A first page should be readable without an account.

4. **When should you write the questions and counts you want from viewers?**  
   a) After the first ten compliments, so the plan can match the praise  
   b) Only after you have already changed the product twice  
   c) Before anyone sees the page, so you do not collect empty praise  
   d) Never; a beautiful theme replaces the need to write anything down  

   **Answer:** c) Before anyone sees the page, so you do not collect empty praise  

   **Explanation:** Planned feedback is decided in advance. If you share first and decide later, you mainly collect compliments. Write the questions before anyone sees the page.

5. **Which planned question is weakest for a first look at a page?**  
   a) What do you think this product does?  
   b) What extra modules should we add and raise money for?  
   c) Would you use this in a normal week — yes, maybe, or no?  
   d) What was confusing on the first screen?  

   **Answer:** b) What extra modules should we add and raise money for?  

   **Explanation:** Understanding, desire, and confusion are the useful layers. Leading with a feature-and-funding wishlist produces a shopping list and loses the plot.
