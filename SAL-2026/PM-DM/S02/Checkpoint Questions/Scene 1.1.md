#### **Scene 1.1**

1. **Which statement best describes a product idea?**  
   a) A wide topic such as campus life with no named user or moment  
   b) A complaint that something should be fixed, with nothing to show  
   c) A specific user, a specific problem, and a small offer you can try  
   d) A pile of extras such as payments, ratings, and a dark mode  

   **Answer:** c) A specific user, a specific problem, and a small offer you can try  

   **Explanation:** A product idea names who hurts, what goes wrong, and a small offer that can be shown. A theme, a complaint, or a feature list is not yet something you can test.

2. **After a chat tool lists many candidate ideas, what is your job?**  
   a) Filter the list and pick one idea you could show without a full product  
   b) Let the model choose, because it invented a market-size ranking  
   c) Keep every idea so none of the generated options is wasted  
   d) Start with payments and maps, then look for a user later  

   **Answer:** a) Filter the list and pick one idea you could show without a full product  

   **Explanation:** The model’s job is volume and sharper wording. You decide. A useful filter asks whether a real person has the pain now and whether a small version can be shown without a full product.

3. **What three checks make up an idea stress-test?**  
   a) Logo colour, font pairing, and animation speed on the page  
   b) Investor names, office lease, and a hiring plan for year two  
   c) Admin dashboard, OTP login, and a city-wide delivery map  
   d) Target user, the painful problem, and a believable why now  

   **Answer:** d) Target user, the painful problem, and a believable why now  

   **Explanation:** Stress-testing asks who the person is, what hurts in a real moment, and why the current workaround is failing now. Decoration and extra features are not the test.

4. **Which statement about “why now” is correct?**  
   a) Why now means the idea is valid simply because chat tools exist  
   b) Why now names why today’s workaround is starting to fail  
   c) Why now is a slogan that replaces naming a real person  
   d) Why now is the same as listing every feature the model suggested  

   **Answer:** b) Why now names why today’s workaround is starting to fail  

   **Explanation:** Why now is a change in the situation — more people, a broken process, a noisy group — not “because AI exists.” AI is a tool for writing the page, not the reason the pain exists.

5. **A one-line value proposition should name which parts?**  
   a) Who it is for, the outcome they get, and the pain they avoid  
   b) Only adjectives such as fast, easy, modern, and seamless  
   c) A company history and the names of future investors  
   d) The programming language planned for a later full product  

   **Answer:** a) Who it is for, the outcome they get, and the pain they avoid  

   **Explanation:** The value line is the sentence a stranger should understand quickly. Buzzwords, history, and later technology do not replace user, job, and pain.
