| Scene 1 — LO1: Select primary marketing channels (social, email, search, or direct outreach) from product type and audience behaviour, using AI to research how that audience uses each channel | Covered | Capture versus generate demand; search versus social; channel versus platform; A-I-C-V-S (audience, intent, complexity, value, scale); product-type × channel matrix; Meta, Snapchat, LinkedIn, email, WhatsApp, calling; audience-behaviour reality check (CFO versus college students; ₹500 headphones versus enterprise cybersecurity). AI used to map channels and compare trade-offs, not to name a single “best” channel. |
| Scene 1 — LO2: Draft a one-page GTM snapshot with AI (segment and positioning already set, channel mix, message adapted to each channel, key activities, metrics) | Covered | Five GTM questions: who, what, where, how, how we will know. FitFataak snapshot: urban office professionals 25–40, unhealthy 4 p.m. snacking, Indian taste + protein + convenience, Instagram and creators to generate demand, Google Search and marketplaces to capture it, retargeting and landing page to convert, email and WhatsApp for reorder, repeatable acquisition as the primary outcome. Stage-wise message jobs (awareness, consideration, decision) taught with FitFataak and a cybersecurity example. |
| Scene 1 — LO3: Check the AI-drafted snapshot for consistency and feasibility, and revise it where it would not hold | Covered | Workflow taught as draft → challenge → revise. Sceptical-growth-leader prompt for audience–channel mismatches, missing stages, over-reliance, and weak assumptions. LinkedIn “professional so it will work” called out as an assumption to test because the platform is expensive. Explicit point that AI is built to agree unless asked to attack the plan. |
| Scene 2 — LO1: Explain how a time-bound launch plan (first awareness and traction) differs from an ongoing plan (retention and growth) | Covered | Launch is a sequence, not day one. Firecracker spike versus a continuous engine: new audiences → engaged → high intent → customers → repeat. Customer questions change across awareness, consideration, decision, experience, and retention. Ongoing plan judged on usage, retention, repeat, churn, and customer lifetime value, not on the launch spike. |
| Scene 2 — LO2: Design the sequence that shifts effort from launch momentum to sustained acquisition and retention | Covered | Phases: foundation (audience, value, tracking, landing page, CRM, sales readiness) → awareness → capture intent → convert → optimise. Week-by-week timeline through scale. Shift budget from social to search when search volume rises; do not switch everything off. Creative fatigue, retargeting, and lookalike “scale winners.” Airtel Business example: do not promote a product the sales team is not ready to sell. |
| Scene 2 — LO3: Define separate success metrics for launch and for ongoing marketing | Covered | Stage metrics taught in place of one company-wide number. Awareness: reach, relevant engagement, video completion, brand search, share of voice, qualified traffic. Consideration: product-page engagement, return visits, content consumption, lead quality, add-to-cart. Decision: conversion, CAC, qualified opportunities, revenue, sales cycle, cart completion. Ongoing: repeat purchase, usage, churn, retention rate, customer lifetime value (₹500/month example → about ₹6,000 a year → about ₹15,000 over three years). Vanity versus diagnostic metrics. Campaign A (CAC ₹500, 10,000 customers, 70% never buy again) versus Campaign B (CAC ₹700, 8,000 customers, 60% buy repeatedly). Day-one signups and press coverage were not the examples used; churn and lifetime value were. |
| Extra — Storytelling can create a market people did not previously feel | Covered | *All Marketers Are Liars*: hair-removal insecurity, quick-commerce habit creation, and the wine-glass seller ($1 glass versus $8–$10 glass; blind test showed no taste difference). Used to separate a strategist who shapes belief from someone who only operates a platform. |
| Extra — In-market versus not-in-market, and high-ticket journeys | Covered | A purchase of a higher-value offer (about ₹5,000–₹10,000 and above) usually ends in capture even if it started from an ad. Low-ticket FitFataak (about ₹60) can convert directly from generated demand. Class purchase path used as the worked journey: reel or friend (generate) → search the name and read reviews (capture) → buy. |
| Extra — Account-based marketing | Covered | Map each target account’s buying stage and change the message. Justified when one account is worth a large contract and switching is rare. Cybersecurity account ladder used (unaware → aware of brand → aware of cloud → using a rival → interested but not confident → ready). |
| Extra — Multi-touch credit | Covered | LinkedIn, website, search, and sales can all touch one deal. Do not give the whole win to the last channel. |
| Extra — Influencer bands, ROAS, lookalikes, RTO, prepaid versus cash on delivery | Covered | Nano, micro, macro, mega, celebrity follower bands. Return on ad spend. Lookalike audiences from converters. Return to origin and the push for more prepaid orders as cart-completion leaks. |
| Extra — Activation, usage, retention, repeat, expansion | Covered | Acquisition is not the end. Activation means the customer actually starts (for a course, attends). Expansion example: a home-services brand adding maid or appliance repair after a plumber visit. |
| Protocol — In-class quizzes | Not part of notes | Two quizzes plus a rapid quiz were run in class. Treated as session protocol, not lecture content. |
