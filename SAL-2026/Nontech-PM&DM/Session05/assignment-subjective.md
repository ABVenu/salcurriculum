# Assignment Subjective

Difficulty: Medium

Questions: 1 practical task

---

## Task: One lamp, one person, one line

### Problem

FoldLite is a study lamp sold online for **₹899**, delivered in Indian metro cities. It does four things you may use as proof:

- A shade keeps the beam on one desk, so the next bed stays dark.
- The light dims by itself after **50 minutes**.
- It folds flat, about the size of a notebook, for a hostel shelf.
- There is no app and no subscription.

Buyers compare it with a phone torch (free, harsh after an hour), the hostel ceiling tube (lights the whole room), a **₹300** lamp that is bright and spills, and a **₹2,500** lamp that needs an app.

Two founders disagree about who it is for. Use only the scorecard below.

| Filter | Hostel students in shared rooms, studying after 10 p.m. | Families buying a lamp for a child who already has a private study table |
|--------|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Market size | High | High |
| Problem strength | High — the ceiling light wakes the roommate; a phone torch strains after a while | Low — most already have a ceiling light or a study lamp |
| Willingness to pay ₹899 | Medium | Medium |
| Reachability | High — hostel groups and student social accounts | Medium |
| Crowding | Medium — many cheap lamps, few that keep light off the next bed | High — stationery and electronics shops already sell study lamps |
| Brand fit | High — fold-flat and a limited beam match a shared room | Low — spill and shelf size matter less in a private room |

Their current positioning draft is:

> For everyone who wants better light, FoldLite is a revolutionary lifestyle platform that empowers modern living.

A sceptical reader sent these notes, and only these:

1. "Everyone" is not a target.
2. "Lifestyle platform" does not say this is a lamp.
3. Nothing in the line is unique to a shared hostel room at night.
4. "Empowers modern living" has no proof. The only proofs you may keep are the shade, the 50-minute dim, the fold-flat body, and ₹899.
5. Do not turn FoldLite into another kind of product. Replace "everyone" with the one group this scorecard actually supports.

The same draft also produced two public lines. The tone you want is calm and practical: a senior who has shared a room, not a meme page and not a coach.

- Line A: "Empowering tomorrow through next-gen illumination for modern living."
- Line B: "Your roommate can sleep. Your chapter does not have to."

You may use a chat tool to draft. Every choice you submit has to follow the scorecard, the proofs, and the sceptic’s notes. Do not invent a new product feature.

### What to submit

Write one note in plain language, in five parts.

1. **Who, and who not.** Choose one column. Name the primary pain in one sentence. Name the column you will not primarily sell to, and at least one filter that makes it the weaker target. Market size is High in both columns, so size alone cannot be your reason. "Everyone who needs light" does not count as a choice.
2. **One persona.** Six lines for the winner: name, city, trigger time, goal, pain, objection. The objection must be something this buyer would actually say about ₹899 or about a lamp they already have.
3. **Two strategy lines.** Write a value line in this shape, and only this shape: For [target audience] who [problem], [product] provides [benefit] because [reason to believe]. Then write a positioning line in this shape, and only this shape: For [target market] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Under them, name one point of parity (a must-have that keeps FoldLite in the lamp category but does not win the choice) and one point of difference (the specific reason to choose it over the phone torch, the ceiling tube, and the ₹300 lamp). The positioning line must match the column you chose in part 1. Use only the proofs listed above. Do not call FoldLite a platform, and do not aim it at everyone.
4. **The brief.** Six fields, one line each: audience, problem, promise, proof, tone, action. The action must be a try or a buy, not "learn more" and not "explore."
5. **Judge, then rewrite.** For Line A and Line B, one sentence each on tone, clarity, and resonance. Then rewrite only the weaker line. The rewrite must be understandable in one breath, name the late-night occasion, include one proof from the list, and end with try or buy. Hide the brand and read it again: if it could advertise a bank, rewrite until it cannot. Do not use "fuel your life," "reimagining," or "next-gen."

## Answer Explanation

### A complete note

**Who, and who not.** Sell to hostel students who study after 10 p.m. in a shared room. The primary pain is that the ceiling light wakes the roommate, and a phone torch is a poor substitute after a while. Do not primarily sell to families who already have a private study table. Market size is High in both columns, and willingness to pay is Medium in both, so size and price do not separate them. Problem strength is Low for those families, and brand fit is Low, because fold-flat and a limited beam solve a shared-room problem they do not have. A large group with a weak problem is a weak target. Crowding in stationery shops makes a price fight a poor backup.

**One persona.** Any name and any metro city are fine if the other fields match this buyer.

- Name: Neel
- City: Pune
- Trigger time: 11:30 p.m., the night before a unit test
- Goal: finish one chapter without leaving the hostel room
- Pain: the ceiling tube wakes the person in the next bed
- Objection: "₹899 is a lot next to a ₹300 lamp"

**Two strategy lines.**

Value line: For hostel students who study after 10 p.m. in a shared room and wake the next bed, FoldLite provides a desk beam that stays on one bunk because the shade limits spill and the light dims after 50 minutes.

Positioning line: For hostel students in metro cities who need to study late without waking a roommate, FoldLite is a foldable study lamp that lights one desk and keeps the next bed dark.

Point of parity: it lights a desk, which a buyer already expects from a lamp. The ₹300 lamp does that too. Missing it would drop FoldLite out of the category. It does not win the choice. The phone torch and the ceiling tube are substitutes, and they are not desk lamps, so "it is a desk lamp" only gets FoldLite onto the shortlist.

Point of difference: the shade keeps the beam off the next bed. The student cares (the roommate is asleep). FoldLite can deliver it (that is what the shade does). A rival’s "bright LED" claim can be copied tomorrow and still spills, so brightness alone is not the difference. The 50-minute dim or the fold-flat body can be named as a supporting proof. The difference that matches the pain is the limited beam.

The founders’ draft fails all five sceptic notes: "everyone," "lifestyle platform," no hostel night, no proof, and no lamp category. The lines above keep the hostel target and only the given proofs.

**The brief.**

- Audience: hostel students in shared rooms
- Problem: late study lights the whole room and wakes the next bed
- Promise: one chapter of light that stays on the desk
- Proof: spill-limiting shade, 50-minute dim, folds flat, ₹899, no app
- Tone: calm and practical
- Action: Try FoldLite

**Judge, then rewrite.**

Line A fails tone (a slogan, not a calm roommate), clarity (no lamp, no hostel, no night), and resonance (nobody’s evening appears). It could sit on a bank or a hospital. It also uses "next-gen," which the brief should have banned.

Line B is closer. Tone is human. Clarity is decent on one read. Resonance is stronger, because a roommate and a chapter are in the line. It still has no proof and no next action, so it is not finished. It is the stronger of the two, so Line A is the one to rewrite.

Rewrite: "Study at 11 p.m. The shade keeps the next bed dark. Try FoldLite."

That is one breath. The occasion is 11 p.m. The proof is the shade. The action is try. With the name hidden, "Study at 11 p.m. The shade keeps the next bed dark," a bank cannot run it.

### Alternative approaches

- Fill the six-field brief before writing the public line, then check the line against the brief. If the shade or the 50-minute dim never appears, the proof field was ignored.
- Or repair the positioning line first, using only the five sceptic notes, and only then judge Line A and Line B. A public line that fights the positioning is not ready.

A note that keeps "everyone," calls FoldLite a lifestyle platform, picks the family column because it is large, treats "it lights a desk" as the reason to buy, or submits "Empowering tomorrow" unchanged does not meet the task.
