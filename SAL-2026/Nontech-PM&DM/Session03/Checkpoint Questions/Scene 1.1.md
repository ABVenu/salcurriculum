#### **Scene 1.1**

1. **What is a test scenario?**  
   a) Praise from the model, plus invented features and a score out of ten  
   b) Who the person is, where they are, what they want, and what they see  
   c) A one-to-ten rating that replaces questions about the intended problem  
   d) Extra modules the model lists so you can skip naming a real moment  

   **Answer:** b) Who the person is, where they are, what they want, and what they see  

   **Explanation:** A test scenario is a short story of a real moment, not a generic “user.” Praise, scores, and extra-module lists are not scenarios.

2. **How should you brief a chat tool before it role-plays a user?**  
   a) Paste the page text only; do not paste hopes or the idea card  
   b) Paste the idea card first so the model already “gets” the problem  
   c) Ask why the product is great, then treat the praise as proof  
   d) Put every user type in one prompt so the voices blend into a committee  

   **Answer:** a) Paste the page text only; do not paste hopes or the idea card  

   **Explanation:** Simulated feedback should react to what is on the screen. Leaking the idea card, asking for praise, or mixing voices is not a test.

3. **Why simulate one user voice per prompt instead of mixing several?**  
   a) One prompt takes longer, so the model will invent a finished market survey  
   b) Many voices in one reply are treated as proof that real people already agree  
   c) Mixing voices is required so the model can give a score out of ten  
   d) Separate prompts keep reactions distinct; mixing them blends the voices  

   **Answer:** d) Separate prompts keep reactions distinct; mixing them blends the voices  

   **Explanation:** One prompt per person keeps the replies from sounding like a committee. Mixing voices, inventing a survey, or forcing a score is not the method.

4. **What should a strong question about the prototype actually test?**  
   a) Whether the logo, colours, and dark mode feel premium enough  
   b) Which extra product lines and funding stories the model can invent  
   c) Whether this version attacks the intended problem, not taste or extras  
   d) Whether a one-to-ten rating can replace yes, maybe, or no plus a reason  

   **Answer:** c) Whether this version attacks the intended problem, not taste or extras  

   **Explanation:** Strong questions check understanding, desire, confusion, and whether the claimed job would actually get easier. Taste, modules, and invented scores do not test that job.

5. **Which statement about simulated user feedback is correct?**  
   a) Detailed chatbot replies equal watching people in the real situation  
   b) It is a rehearsal filter, not proof that real people will use the offer  
   c) If the model writes a long review, you can skip any later real-user test  
   d) A simulated “no” is harder to hear than a real person saying no to a friend  

   **Answer:** b) It is a rehearsal filter, not proof that real people will use the offer  

   **Explanation:** Simulated replies help you catch fuzzy headlines and weak next steps. They miss real thumbs, trust, and social cost, so detail is not evidence.
