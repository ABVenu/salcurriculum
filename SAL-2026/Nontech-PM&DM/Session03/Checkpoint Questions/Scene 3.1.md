#### **Scene 3.1**

1. **What is a rationale for a product change?**  
   a) Saying we made it nicer, with no finding and no link to the intended problem  
   b) A funding story and a long future roadmap instead of the actual page edits  
   c) A claim that simulated voices already count as a finished national user survey  
   d) The reasoned why for what you changed or kept, serving the user and the MVP  

   **Answer:** d) The reasoned why for what you changed or kept, serving the user and the MVP  

   **Explanation:** A rationale names what you changed or kept and why that choice serves the user and the MVP. “Nicer,” funding stories, and fake surveys are not a rationale.

2. **Which order should a short presentation of a refined offer follow?**  
   a) Open with a funding ask, a long module list, then a claim that users already love it  
   b) Problem, first page, what you heard, what changed, what you parked, what to learn next  
   c) Start with colour history and logo variants, then ask listeners to ignore the MVP boundary  
   d) Use adjectives and a sales pitch, with no mention of simulated versus real as the source  

   **Answer:** b) Problem, first page, what you heard, what changed, what you parked, what to learn next  

   **Explanation:** The spine is who and the problem, the first page, what you heard (labelled as simulated if it was), what changed, what you parked, and what to learn next from real people.

3. **What is a change log used for when presenting a refined prototype?**  
   a) A mood board of visual themes with no source attached to any of the edits  
   b) A pile of compliments only, with no record of features you refused to build  
   c) A table that maps each edit or refusal to the feedback that prompted it  
   d) A hidden list of taste-only edits you will not mention when you present  

   **Answer:** c) A table that maps each edit or refusal to the feedback that prompted it  

   **Explanation:** Each row is a change or a refusal tied to a source. Empty rows, hidden taste edits, and compliment piles are not a change log.

4. **You changed a line with no finding behind it. How should that row be treated?**  
   a) Label it taste and be ready to undo it if anyone asks why that line exists  
   b) Present it as if two simulated voices had already stalled on that same line  
   c) Hide it from the log so the presented story looks fully based on evidence  
   d) Treat it as stronger than a finding because you preferred the new wording  

   **Answer:** a) Label it taste and be ready to undo it if anyone asks why that line exists  

   **Explanation:** A change with no finding is taste. Label it and be ready to undo it. Do not dress it up as evidence or hide it.

5. **What is a trade-off when you refine an early product?**  
   a) Claiming the page is both a tiny test and a finished live product at once  
   b) Apologising for every cut without naming what the cut protected for the user  
   c) Listing only what you built and never naming the features you refused to ship  
   d) Accepting a downside in one area so a more important outcome stays protected  

   **Answer:** d) Accepting a downside in one area so a more important outcome stays protected  

   **Explanation:** A trade-off names both sides: what you chose and what you gave up, usually to protect clarity, honesty, or MVP fit under a time limit. A false claim that you have both is not a trade-off.
