## Task: Multichannel copy sprint for FitFataak (AI-assisted, human-edited)

### Context

**FitFataak** is a protein-rich snack positioned as **evening hunger solved without giving up taste or piling on empty calories**, with a distinctive **protein bhujia** flavour. You are **not** redoing positioning or GTM from earlier sessions—you are producing **usable channel assets** from the spine below.

**Spine (use consistently):**  
*Evening hunger solved without compromising taste or piling on empty calories.*

**Audience for this sprint:** urban professionals **25–40** who already know snack brands but have **not** bought FitFataak; they are **problem-aware** (4 p.m. hunger, guilt about fried namkeen) but **brand-unaware**.

Use any AI chat tool you already have access to. Show your prompts and paste outputs (or redacted excerpts). You own **truth, tone, and publishability**.

### What you must produce (in one Google Doc)

1. **WHO–DON’T brief (your words):** Fill **WHO, WHY, WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, DO, DON’T** for **one** asset: a **cart-abandonment email** (user added FitFataak to cart, left without paying). Keep DON’T concrete (banned words, no unverified health claims, no competitor names).

2. **Two-channel generation:** Using your brief plus the spine, prompt AI for **(a)** that email (≤120 words, customer-out opening, one primary CTA) and **(b)** one **WhatsApp** message to the same segment (respect ~50 words, one CTA, no formal salutation). After each draft, add **one sentence** explaining what you changed because of the **channel** (not just word count).

3. **Iteration loop on LinkedIn:** Prompt for **five** LinkedIn feed hooks on the evening-snack tension. Mark your **top two**. Run a **directed** second prompt (not “make it better”) that rewrites those two for this audience with a stated constraint (e.g., no fear-based guilt, max 14 words). Then run a **sceptical reader** prompt on the rewritten pair and keep **only one** hook you would actually post—with one line on why.

4. **Remove AI smell:** Paste this AI paragraph into your doc unchanged, then use a structured edit (your words or an adapted “remove AI smell” style prompt) and show the **after** version in **two sentences max** plus a **three-item** list of the most important edits (e.g., buzzword removed, invented stat flagged, customer-out restored):

   > In today’s rapidly evolving wellness landscape, FitFataak is thrilled to announce a revolutionary, best-in-class protein snack that seamlessly unlocks unprecedented health outcomes. Trust us—99% of professionals already made the switch. The future of snacking starts now.

5. **Mini fact-check ledger:** From your **email draft in part 2**, list every **number, superlative, or health-style claim** in a small table with columns **Claim | Verified / Unverifiable / Delete** and one line on what source tier you would use (docs, site, or delete). Add **one** human nuance sentence (Gurgaon–Goa-style specificity) that AI did not invent but you would add before send.

## Answer Explanation

### Ideal walkthrough

1. **WHO–DON’T:** WHO = urban pros 25–40, cart abandoners, problem-aware / brand-unaware. WHY = 4 p.m. hunger vs fried default. WHAT = protein bhujia, taste without empty calories. WHERE = email. WHEN = post-abandon, same day or next day. DO = low-friction return to cart or short read. DON’T = “best-in-class,” unverified “high protein %,” competitor shots, fake urgency discounts.

2. **Two-channel:** Email structure should follow subject/hook/value/proof/CTA pattern at appropriate length; WhatsApp must be **context → value → action**, ~50 words, one CTA, no “Dear Customer.” Channel explanation sentences must cite **behaviour** (inbox intent vs intimate chat), not only “shorter.”

3. **Iteration:** First prompt produces variety; second prompt names **which options** and **diagnosis** (generic, guilt, product-heavy). Sceptical-reader step should call out distrust triggers; final single hook should be believable and on-brand for FitFataak.

4. **AI smell edit:** After text should drop thrilled/revolutionary/unlock/99% trust line; replace with concrete evening-snack situation. Ledger should flag **99%** and **best-in-class / unprecedented** as delete or unverifiable unless sourced.

5. **Fact-check:** Any “protein per serving” or “healthiest” line from the model without pack label → Unverifiable or Delete. Human nuance example: specific desk scenario (“metro ride home at 7 p.m.”) without fake stats.

### Alternative approaches

- Students may use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or similar; tool choice does not matter if prompts and outputs are visible.
- Part 2 may include a **marketplace listing** instead of WhatsApp only if the student swaps part (b) to listing and explains title/bullets/CTA architecture—but default rubric expects WhatsApp as specified.

### What fails the task

- Same paragraph pasted for email and WhatsApp with no structural adaptation.
- WhatsApp over 50 words, formal salutation, or multiple CTAs.
- Part 3 stops at “make it punchier” with no sceptical-reader step.
- Part 4 leaves invented **99%** stat in the “after” copy.
- Part 5 empty or marks all claims Verified without source reasoning.
- Doc link not set to **Anyone with the link** can view.
