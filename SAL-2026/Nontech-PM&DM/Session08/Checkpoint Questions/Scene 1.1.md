#### **Scene 1.1**

1. **A team converts a LinkedIn post into WhatsApp by simply reducing its word count. What is the most important weakness?**  
   a) The copy may preserve a structure that does not suit WhatsApp behaviour  
   b) The copy may lose keywords required for accurate social-media distribution  
   c) The message may become too short to communicate any meaningful proposition  
   d) The message may require an additional headline before it can be delivered  

   **Answer:** a) The copy may preserve a structure that does not suit WhatsApp behaviour  

   **Explanation:** Each channel needs a different communication architecture, not just a shorter word count.

2. **A B2B marketer is creating the first email for a problem-aware prospect who does not know the brand. Which structure is strongest?**  
   a) Company credentials → product catalogue → pricing → meeting request  
   b) Customer context → relevant problem → value → low-friction next step  
   c) Product features → competitor weakness → discount → purchase request  
   d) Brand history → market statistics → product portfolio → contact details  

   **Answer:** b) Customer context → relevant problem → value → low-friction next step  

   **Explanation:** Strong outbound email leads with the customer's situation and a clear, low-friction CTA.

3. **An AI-generated InMail is personalised with the recipient's name and company but still performs poorly. What should be examined next?**  
   a) Whether the message demonstrates relevance beyond superficial identity variables  
   b) Whether the recipient's name appears more frequently throughout the communication  
   c) Whether the company description contains sufficient technical detail about the product  
   d) Whether the message uses the same structure as the highest-performing email campaign  

   **Answer:** a) Whether the message demonstrates relevance beyond superficial identity variables  

   **Explanation:** InMail must answer "why you, why now" with context the reader recognises, not only name and company tags.

4. **Why is an iterative prompt usually stronger than repeatedly asking AI to "make it better"?**  
   a) It narrows the model toward specific weaknesses and desired communication choices  
   b) It increases the probability that AI will generate substantially longer marketing copy  
   c) It prevents the model from changing any words that appeared in the initial response  
   d) It guarantees that the final draft will outperform copy written without generative AI  

   **Answer:** a) It narrows the model toward specific weaknesses and desired communication choices  

   **Explanation:** Diagnose what failed (generic, wrong audience, too product-heavy), then give directed feedback instead of vague "make it better."

5. **A marketer creates email, WhatsApp and call scripts from one positioning statement. What should remain most consistent?**  
   a) The sentence structure and CTA wording used across each customer interaction  
   b) The central customer value while execution adapts to channel behaviour  
   c) The total amount of product information communicated within every channel  
   d) The communication length so customers receive equivalent detail everywhere  

   **Answer:** b) The central customer value while execution adapts to channel behaviour  

   **Explanation:** Same strategic spine; change structure, length, opening, proof, CTA, and tone by channel.
