## Q1 (MCQ, Easy)

Ananya lives in a paying-guest house in Bengaluru. Every weekday morning the shared microwave is occupied, leftover dabbas sit inside, and people miss the office cab.

Which line is a product idea she could actually show this week?

A. “PG kitchens are messy, and someone should fix them soon.”  
B. “A smart living platform for all urban residents in India.”  
C. “Page one must include payments, ratings, dark mode, and maps.”  
D. “PG residents book a 10-minute microwave slot before the cab.”

**Correct Answer:** D

**Answer Explanation:**  
A product idea names a specific user, a specific problem, and a small offer someone can try. Booking a microwave slot before the cab is an offer Ananya can put on one page this week.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: This is a complaint. It names a gripe, not an offer you can show.  
- B: This is a theme. It is too wide, with no person and no moment of pain.  
- C: These are extra features. A feature pile without a user and a pain is not an idea you can test.

## Q2 (MCQ, Easy)

Farhan asks a chat tool for eight small product ideas around the college photocopy-shop queue. The reply mixes a city-wide printing marketplace, a dark-mode request for the college portal, and a one-counter token page.

What should Farhan do next?

A. Keep and build all eight ideas so none of the generated list is wasted.  
B. Drop team-sized ideas, then pick one he can explain in 20 seconds.  
C. Let the model pick, since it added a ranked table of invented market sizes.  
D. Add payments and rider pickup first, then look for a real user later.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Answer Explanation:**  
The model’s job is volume and sharper wording. Farhan’s job is choice. A useful filter asks whether a real person has the pain this week and whether a small version can be shown without a full product.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: Most of a brainstorm should die. Building every option wastes the week.  
- C: Invented market-size numbers are not a reason to let the model decide. Delete those lines.  
- D: Payments and riders are extras. They do not replace a named user and a small thing you can show.

## Q3 (MCQ, Easy)

Sneha is writing one sentence for a page that helps evening-shift nurses at a small clinic skip the long report-collection queue.

Which sentence is a usable one-line value proposition?

A. “SlotNote helps evening-shift nurses book a report pickup without the morning queue.”  
B. “MediSync is an AI-powered revolutionary healthcare ecosystem for every clinic.”  
C. “Fast, easy, modern, and seamless report handling for every clinic visitor.”  
D. “We will raise funding next quarter and hire a full in-house design team.”

**Correct Answer:** A

**Answer Explanation:**  
A value proposition names who the offer is for, the job they get done, and the pain they avoid. A classmate should be able to repeat that line after hearing it once.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- B: Buzzwords such as ecosystem and revolutionary hide the user, the job, and the pain.  
- C: Adjectives are a slogan, not a value line. They do not say who or why.  
- D: Fundraising and hiring are later company work, not a sentence a stranger can act on.

## Q4 (MCQ, Easy)

Rohit is building a one-page site on Google Sites for neighbourhood cricket-ground booking. He already has a headline, a value line, and three short steps.

What is still missing before the page can be shown?

A. A twenty-page blog, an About page, a Team page, and a long company history.  
B. A login wall, a payment gateway, and an admin dashboard on page one.  
C. One working button that opens a small form or a chat he controls.  
D. Fake 4.9-star ratings that claim two thousand users already joined.

**Correct Answer:** C

**Answer Explanation:**  
A first landing page has one story and one main action. The call-to-action must name the next step and must actually open a destination Rohit controls, such as a tiny Google Form or a chat link.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: Extra pages split attention and are out of scope for a first prototype.  
- B: Login, payments, and an admin panel belong to a full product, not this first page.  
- D: Invented ratings are false social proof. They do not replace a working next step.

## Q5 (MCQ, Moderate)

Priya wants to help apartment residents who currently shout in a noisy WhatsApp group to book the shared visitor-parking slot. Two cars now arrive for the same slot, and the watchman gets blamed.

Which “why now” actually stress-tests the idea?

A. Chat tools exist this year, so any parking idea is automatically valid.  
B. She cannot name who parks or when the fight happens, so she should add more features instead.  
C. “Everyone with a phone” is already a sharp enough target user to test.  
D. More flats now share two visitor slots, and the group is noisy and double-booked.

**Correct Answer:** D

**Answer Explanation:**  
“Why now” names why the old workaround is failing at this time — more people, the same scarce slot, a group nobody updates. That is a change in the situation, not a slogan.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: “Because AI exists” is not a why now. A chat tool can help write the page; it is not the reason the pain exists.  
- B: If the user and moment are unclear, the idea is not sharp yet. Adding features hides the hole instead of repairing it.  
- C: “Everyone with a phone” is too wide. A stress-test needs a person you could actually meet, plus when the pain happens.

## Q6 (MCQ, Moderate)

Vikram spent three days picking a logo colour and adding a dark theme for a canteen-token page. The headline says “Welcome to TokenHub.” There are two buttons, Login and Shop now. The only form field asks for a parent phone number and a password. Nobody outside his friend group has opened the link.

Which judgement is correct?

A. The page is already good enough to test, because the paid-looking template and dark theme prove a stranger will understand the offer.  
B. The page fails the testable gate: unclear offer, extra buttons, and login plus private fields.  
C. He should add UPI checkout, an admin dashboard, and a second “Our Story” page before anyone sees the link.  
D. Friends clapping at the dark theme is enough evidence that a new person understands the offer.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Answer Explanation:**  
A prototype is good enough to test when a new person can understand the offer, see one next step, and give a reaction you can write down. Vikram’s headline names no job, two buttons split attention, login tests signup friction instead of the offer, and extra private fields do not belong on a first waitlist.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: An expensive-looking theme is polish. Looks do not replace a clear offer and a working next step.  
- C: Payments, dashboards, and extra pages are early polish that delay the test. They do not fix understanding.  
- D: Friends clapping is not the check. The check is whether a new person understands and can act.

## Q7 (MSQ, Moderate)

Kavya is assembling a first prototype for a coaching-class doubt-queue page. She has her one-liner ready.

Which of the following belong on this first prototype?

A. Login, payments, an admin dashboard, and a city-wide map should all sit on page one.  
B. Four bands on the page: headline, value line, three short steps, and one call-to-action.  
C. A 20-box survey plus Aadhaar and a parent phone number on the waitlist.  
D. A tiny 3-field form or chat link, then a phone check that the button works.

**Correct Answers:** B, D

**Answer Explanation:**  
A first prototype is a wireframe or one landing page on a visual website builder. The page uses four bands: headline, value line, how it works, and one working call-to-action. The waitlist is part of the prototype, so the button must open something real and tiny. A phone preview confirms the headline is readable and the button works.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: Login, payments, dashboards, and maps are a full product. They hide the one job the page should test.  
- C: A huge survey and official identity or extra private numbers do not belong on a first waitlist.

## Q8 (MSQ, Moderate)

A chat tool drafts extra lines and picture ideas for Imran’s tailor-pickup page for college uniforms.

Which actions keep the supporting material honest?

A. Keep a short “who this is for / is not” line so the wrong person does not join.  
B. Delete invented testimonials, ratings, and “trusted by” counts he did not supply.  
C. Prefer a photo he can take today, or a 3-icon row, over a film-style banner.  
D. Treat a generated smiling-customer photo as live proof that two hundred students already use the service.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C

**Answer Explanation:**  
Supporting copy helps a visitor decide without inventing new facts. A short “who this is / is not” line is useful. Visuals should explain, not decorate: a real photo or a 3-icon row is enough. Any count, quote, or rating the model invented must be deleted.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- D: A generated picture is only a placeholder. Claiming it is a real customer, or inventing a user count, is the same class of error as publishing unchecked chatbot facts.

## Q9 (MSQ, Hard)

Neha has a live one-page site for a shared study-room booking in her apartment. She has not shown it yet. She is filling a collection sheet.

Which plans are correct for a first look?

A. Write three questions in advance: what they think it does, yes / maybe / no on use, and what confused them first.  
B. Lead with “What features should I add?” and “Looks premium, no?” so she collects a shopping list and empty praise.  
C. Tally opens, form fills, and yes / maybe / no — and remember those counts are not proof of a paying business.  
D. Plan two hundred fake responses and collect Aadhaar numbers so the first-look sheet looks official.

**Correct Answers:** A, C

**Answer Explanation:**  
Planned feedback is written before anyone sees the page, otherwise you only collect compliments. The useful layers are understanding, desire, and confusion. Simple counts (opens, form fills, yes / maybe / no) tell you whether people looked and whether the call-to-action felt worth it. They do not prove a business.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- B: “Looks premium?” is empty praise. “What features should I add?” produces a shopping list and loses the plot.  
- D: Fake volume and official identity numbers do not belong on a first-look sheet. Plan a small, real set of people you can actually reach.

## Q10 (MSQ, Hard)

A classmate hands Tara this brief: “An AI-powered smart commute ecosystem for all city travellers.” She wants a testable first version for office-goers who miss the 8:10 shared auto at one junction because nobody updates the paper list on the tea stall.

Which statements correctly apply the path from raw idea to a testable prototype?

A. Keep “for all city travellers” and add rider tracking, payments, and ratings so the hole in the idea stays hidden.  
B. Rewrite the brief as one person, one painful moment, one page with no login, and a list of what it must not include.  
C. Park logo animation, dark mode, and a second “Our story” page; keep a headline a stranger can repeat and a working waitlist form.  
D. Use the chat tool for many options and sharper wording, but she still picks; if a tough question kills the idea, shrink the user or switch.

**Correct Answers:** B, C, D

**Answer Explanation:**  
A fluffy line such as “smart commute ecosystem for all city travellers” is a theme, not an idea. Repair it by naming a person, a moment, a one-page first version, and what must stay out. AI can list options and tighten wording; Tara still chooses. Early polish (animation, dark mode, extra pages) can wait. What you need to test is a clear offer and a working next step. A failed stress-test is useful: shrink the user or switch idea.

**Why other options are wrong:**  
- A: “Everyone” is too wide. Adding extras to hide a weak user, problem, or why now is a hiding move, not a refine.
