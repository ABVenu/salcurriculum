#### **Scene 2.1**

1. **Which of the following is Generative AI rather than recognition or decision AI?**  
   a) Matching a stored fingerprint pattern to unlock a locked phone  
   b) Writing a new workshop invitation that did not exist before  
   c) Sorting an incoming message into a junk or inbox folder  
   d) Confirming that the camera image matches the enrolled user  

   **Answer:** b) Writing a new workshop invitation that did not exist before  

   **Explanation:** Generative AI creates new content from a request. Unlock, spam sorting, and face matching recognise or decide; they do not write a new document.

2. **What changed most in everyday writing after generative chat tools became widely available?**  
   a) Official payment and booking systems were replaced by chatbot guesses  
   b) Truth and high-stakes advice became the model’s only responsibility  
   c) Learners stopped sitting exams because the tool submitted the papers  
   d) Faster first drafts made briefing and checking into core skills  

   **Answer:** d) Faster first drafts made briefing and checking into core skills  

   **Explanation:** Generative AI made creating text cheap and fast. Official systems, truth, permissions, and exams did not move to the model. The human still briefs, edits, and owns the send button.

3. **What is a prompt in a generative AI tool?**  
   a) A score the model awards itself after it finishes a reply  
   b) A certificate that proves generated text is factually true  
   c) The instruction you give a generative model to shape its output  
   d) The cooling fan that runs while the computer uses the model  

   **Answer:** c) The instruction you give a generative model to shape its output  

   **Explanation:** A prompt is the brief you give the system — the request that conditions what it should generate next. The model cannot see unspoken goals.

4. **What does the Role–Context–Format approach ask you to specify?**  
   a) Role, situation and limits, and the required shape of the output  
   b) Only the font colour and the printer tray for the document  
   c) A promise that the model will never output an incorrect number  
   d) The brand of graphics card used while the model was trained  

   **Answer:** a) Role, situation and limits, and the required shape of the output  

   **Explanation:** Role is who to be, Context is the situation plus constraints the model cannot know, and Format is how the answer should look (list, table, email, and so on).

5. **A prompt names the audience, the goal, and the exact scope of the work. Which component is this?**  
   a) Retrieval: pulling live attendance from the official student portal  
   b) Hallucination: adding a fake survey so the answer looks complete  
   c) Specificity: naming the audience, the goal, and the task’s scope  
   d) Tokenization: splitting the prompt into separate hardware devices  

   **Answer:** c) Specificity: naming the audience, the goal, and the task’s scope  

   **Explanation:** Specificity makes the task exact — who it is for, what to achieve, and how far to go. Constraints are limits such as length or “do not invent numbers.” Format is the shape of the reply.
