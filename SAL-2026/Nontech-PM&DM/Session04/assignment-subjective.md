# Assignment Subjective

Difficulty: Medium

Questions: 1 practical task

---

## Task: What should the group repeat next month?

### Problem

A mixed group of students is running a free weekend session. Classmates from any course can join. The subject of the session does not matter.

They invited people in four ordinary ways. Some people showed up. Some of those came back the next weekend. The group meets on Monday and must choose **one** change for next month. They do not have a budget table, and they do not know why a person stayed home.

A classmate pasted the tables into a chat tool and wants the group to adopt the reply below. You will check that reply against the tables, then write the note the group should actually use.

You may use a chat tool to draft. Every percentage in your note must be worked out by you. Use only the tables and the timing note.

### Table 1 — Who was invited, and who showed up

| How they heard | Invited | Showed up |
|----------------|---------|-----------|
| A teacher mentioned it in class | 400 | 280 |
| A poster near the gate | 50 | 10 |
| A message in the class group | 250 | 100 |
| A friend asked them in person | 300 | 210 |

Show-up rate = showed up ÷ invited.

### Table 2 — Of the people who showed up, who came back the next weekend

| How they heard | Showed up (count) | Came back next weekend |
|----------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| A teacher mentioned it in class | 280 | 84 |
| A poster near the gate | 10 | 4 |
| A message in the class group | 100 | 50 |
| A friend asked them in person | 210 | 63 |

Come-back rate = came back ÷ showed up.

### Table 3 — Class-group invites, week by week

| Week | People invited in the class group |
|------|------------------------------------|
| 1 | 40 |
| 2 | 42 |
| 3 | 44 |
| 4 | 80 |
| 5 | 44 |

These five weeks are the same 250 class-group invites as in Table 1.

### Timing note

The poster is put up at the gate on Friday at 6 pm, after classes have ended. Most students have already left. Nobody is asked to redesign the poster.

### The reply your classmate wants the group to adopt

> The class-group message is the best way to invite people: 100 showed up out of 250, so the show-up rate is 100%. The poster failed, at 10%, because the poster looks dull — stop using posters. People from the class group came back more, which proves the class group causes people to return, so invite everyone only through the class group. Week 4 had 80 class-group invites, so invites doubled this month. Improve attendance.

### What to submit

Write one note in plain language. A classmate who has not seen these tables should be able to follow it. Use these four parts.

1. **Corrected rates.** For each of the four ways of inviting people, write the show-up rate and the come-back rate you calculated. Then write the two rates in the chat reply that are wrong, and the rates you got instead.
2. **What you would not do.** Name the actions in the reply you would not let the group take. For each one, say the plain reason: the count is too small, the gap is too narrow to matter, it is one unusual week, the reply invents a reason, or two things happened together but one has not been shown to cause the other.
3. **The one change to try.** One specific change for next month. It must be something the group can actually do. Do not write only "improve attendance." Do not choose a change the tables cannot support.
4. **How you will know.** What you will compare, for how long, and which number will tell the group whether the change worked.

### Submission instruction

- Type the answer in the answer box.

## Answer Explanation

### Rates

Show-up (Table 1):

- Teacher in class: 280 ÷ 400 = 70%
- Poster near the gate: 10 ÷ 50 = 20%, not 10%
- Class-group message: 100 ÷ 250 = 40%, not 100%
- Friend in person: 210 ÷ 300 = 70%
- Overall: (280 + 10 + 100 + 210) ÷ (400 + 50 + 250 + 300) = 600 ÷ 1,000 = 60%

Come-back (Table 2):

- Teacher: 84 ÷ 280 = 30%
- Poster: 4 ÷ 10 = 40%, on only 10 people
- Class group: 50 ÷ 100 = 50%
- Friend: 63 ÷ 210 = 30%

Weeks (Table 3): 40, 42, 44, 80, 44. They sum to 250. Four weeks sit between 40 and 44. Week 4 is one high week. The month did not double.

### What a strong note does

**Corrected rates.** Teacher and friend both show up at 70%, on 400 and 300 invites. Those two are close. The class group shows up at 40% on 250 invites, a wide gap against the 70% ways. The poster shows up at 20% on only 50 invites. Come-back is 50% for the class group (50 of 100), against 30% for the teacher (84 of 280) and 30% for a friend (63 of 210).

The reply's 100% and 10% are both wrong. 100 ÷ 250 = 40%. 10 ÷ 50 = 20%. The counts 100, 250, 10, and 50 are real. The percentages made from them are not. "Week 4 had 80" is a real count used to claim the month doubled, which the other four weeks do not support.

**What not to do.**

- Do not stop the posters because they "look dull." That reason is not in the tables. The poster goes up at 6 pm after classes, which is a timing fact, not a design fact. Fifty invites and ten arrivals are also too small a count to ban the method.
- Do not drop the teacher mention or the friend invite. Both show up at 70% on large counts.
- Do not say the class group causes people to come back. Coming back more often (50% versus 30%) only shows the two things happened together.
- Do not treat Week 4 as a new normal, and do not staff or scale the class group because of that one week.
- "Improve attendance" is not a change.

**One change that fits the tables.** For the next four weekends, send one reminder on Saturday morning to people who were invited in the class group and have not shown up yet. Leave the teacher mention and the friend invite in place. Leave the poster in place until there is a larger count.

Other specific tests are acceptable if they aim at the class group's 40% show-up, name what the group will do, and do not ban a small poster count or call come-back a proven cause.

**How you will know.** Compare the class-group show-up rate over those four weekends with the current 40%. Also watch the come-back rate, so a higher show-up is not only people who never return.

### Alternative approaches

- Work out the eight rates on paper first. Ask a chat tool to turn only those checked rates into sentences, then delete any sentence you cannot point to in the tables.
- Or underline the classmate's reply first: true count, wrong rate, made-up reason, one unusual week, "this caused that." Write the Monday note only after that pass.

A note that repeats 100% or 10%, bans posters for looking dull, drops the 70% invite methods, or stops at "improve attendance" does not meet the task.
